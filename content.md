Today I met with a man named Stephan S. He grew up in a household with his father, sister, four aunties, and a grandmother. His father was often not home and very absent when present. 
There was a strong force of Jehovah Witness influence among his grandmother and aunties. Stephan's father did not subscribe to this cult and as a result there was a rift in family bonds. 
Stephan's mother left when he was very young (I think 5-6), so his aunties and grandmother would watch over him when his father was away for work, which was often. I asked Stephen to tell me 
what his friendships were like and he replied "I didn't have any, I wasn't allowed to have friends". I dug deeper "Can you tell me who you were close with then?". "My sister was my best friend,
but I played a lot of video games...".
Stephan was also home-schooled from grade 1 onward, adding to his isolation. He and his sister grew up in a very secluded environment and developed unhealthy relational patterns to others, but 
Stephan admitted he didn't really have a framework or vocabulary to understand this at the time. He described relationships as having "no depth" and we spoke at length about the effects this would
have in later life. I asked many clarifying questions about loneliness and how Stephan managed being in this environment. Stephan said "I don't think I ever heard the words 'I love you' while growing up."
Nor did he know physical affection. So, dear reader, you will understand that Stephan was without much guidance in how to be affectionate in his relationships. Now, he sees chaotic situations and can maintain
his composure quite easily, but not because he is in control of his emotions. Rather, his baseline emotional level is so low that emotional response is inhibited, regardless of situation.
He admitted to me that when his sister was 18 she became pregnant, gave birth to a little girl named Haven, and shared a room with the two closest ladies in his life. Haven would soon celebrate her 1st birthday when one night she made a disturbing squealing wimper
as they all slept. Immediately Stephan's sister was awake and tending to Haven. The aunt's and grandmother were roused by this commotion and came to assist. 911 was called and Haven was in sever medical distress. 
Stephan was left at home while his sister, Haven, and the other women left for the hospital. He received a phone call later in the night to inform him that his niece, the little girl he had spent the last year with, 
had died. Stephan described his hands shaking violently and the kindest act he remembered of his aunty when she brought him a glass of water. Stephan recounted trying to bring the glass to his lips amidst the tremors. 

Investigators came that night to determine what had happened, so they asked the family to stay out of the house as the investigation was ongoing. Stephan remembered "...pacing in the drive way and along the sidewalk
when this was all happening. The investigators came to ask me questions, but I was emotionless. I remember just having flat responses to their questions....something switched in me on that night. I turned everything off."

My co-worker Milan began a theological discussion with me regarding 1st century Judaism today (Nov 13, 2026). He has previously tried to criticize me and my "protestant views" based on his catholic education. 
He is a PhD in Biology and quite knowledgeable, so I must bring my strongest reasoning to bare while in these conversations. He will say the most verifiably dumb things in theological conversations
while trying to sound totally convinced of it. In summation of todays topic we ended up in the deity, truth, and motives of God in Jesus Christ's life. I asked Milan to cut to the consequential meaning
of his own faith and to stop making these conversations about intellectual peeing matches where he tries to out gun me via quotes of catholic doctrine and theologians. I appealed to words of Christ in
the Gospels and quoted several of them for him, but focused on asking Milan to tell me if Jesus actually was raised from the dead and exited the tomb where his dead body was lain. Milan replied "I don't know".
Milan then proceeded to tell me that it wasn't necessary that Christ actually was raised from the dead, that the catholic doctrine on the resurrection did not claim the resurrection as a miracle, and that 
Jews and Christians were able to find common ground in the teaching of Christ's "Love your neighbour as yourself." I asked him once again to confirm that those words belong to Christ and he said yes. I asked
him to confirm that he believed those words to enrich his life. He said yes. I then asked him to consider that perhaps the other teachings of Christ were also true. He was resistant and unsure of this. At this point
I narrowed my intent to the motive of God in Jesus. I talked about how God chose to become a man named Jesus (among other names) and become acquainted with the human condition _for our sake___. Jesus knew what it was
to suffer and when he encountered suffering in others he healed them. I asked Milan to provide an explanation as to why Jesus did this and he gave a vague response about Jewish ethics requiring it. I replied "Do you think
that Jesus cared for those people he healed?" Milan responded flippantly "I think that maybe he cared for them." Intently I replied "Would you be willing to say he loved the people he healed?" and Milan said yes. 
This was my final flourish of the conversation. "Milan I would suggest to you that God's motive is love, that Jesus taught many things, but we sometimes choose to only follow some of them. If I truly tried to live out 
Jesus's teachings like 'Turn the other cheek when struck', 'go the extra mile', and 'it is better to give than to receive.' I think that my life would be better for it _because of love._ My whole goal was to have Milan
consider that perhaps the words of God were meant to be more than intellectual play. Perhaps they were meant to heal.

So these are two stories from one day in my life. The tale of Stephan happened after the conversation with Milan. Now I am left with these thoughts and I cannot tell them to you. I cannot be surprised by your perspective, or 
encouraged by you. I cannot hear anymore of your stories after you have listened to mine. 
This is why I miss you and I have missed you and I will miss you. I still want to tell you everything and I love that feeling.
